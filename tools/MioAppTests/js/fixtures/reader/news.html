Scientists Find Water on Distant Moon

By Lois Lane
Tweet Share

A team of astronomers announced on Tuesday that they had found strong evidence of liquid water beneath the icy crust of a distant moon, a discovery that could reshape the search for life beyond Earth.

The researchers, working with data from a probe that flew past the moon last year, measured subtle changes in its gravitational field. Those changes, they said, are best explained by a global ocean, roughly as deep as the Pacific, sloshing beneath the surface.

"This is one of the most exciting results of the decade," said Dr. Ada Park, the lead author of the study, published in the journal Planetary Science. "We have been waiting a long time for this kind of evidence, and it opens many new questions."

What comes next

The team hopes to send a dedicated lander within the next fifteen years. Such a mission, however, would be expensive, technically difficult, and would need international cooperation to succeed, officials warned.

Other scientists urged caution, noting that the gravitational data could also be explained by unusual rock formations, and that independent confirmation would be needed before any firm conclusion could be drawn.
